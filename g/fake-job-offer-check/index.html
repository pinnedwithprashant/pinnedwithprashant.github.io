Check a job offer for scam red flags with ChatGPT or Gemini

Paste a job offer into ChatGPT or Gemini and ask for five red-flag checks. Then confirm the job on the company's own website. If you already paid, report it at cybercrime.gov.in or call 1930.

Who it's for

Anyone in India who got a job offer by WhatsApp, SMS, email or a call, especially freshers and people looking at jobs abroad.

How to try it

  1. Copy the full offer: the message, the email, the sender's address and any link.
  2. Remove your own personal details (Aadhaar, PAN, bank details) before you paste anything.
  3. Paste it into ChatGPT or Gemini with the prompt below.
  4. Read the red flags the AI lists. Treat any request for money or bank details as a stop sign.
  5. Open the company's own website yourself (not the link in the message) and look for the same job on its careers page.
  6. If you already paid or shared documents, report at cybercrime.gov.in or call 1930.

Prompts to copy

Prompt
Here is a job offer I received. Check it for scam red flags. 1) Does it ask me for any money or bank details? 2) Is it from a free email address instead of the company's own domain? 3) Is the pay unusually high for the work? 4) Does it pressure me to decide fast? 5) What should I search for on the company's official careers page to confirm it? List each red flag with the exact line that triggered it. Offer: <paste here>

Good to know

  • The AI can only spot warning signs. It cannot prove an offer is real.
  • AAI says it never takes payment at any stage and announces vacancies only on www.aai.aero (PIB, 27 Mar 2026).
  • Sanchar Saathi lists money or bank-detail requests, free email domains and too-good-to-be-true offers as warning signs.
  • Never paste your Aadhaar, PAN, bank or OTP details into any chatbot.
  • For jobs abroad, be extra careful: 6,998 Indians were rescued from scam compounds in Cambodia, Laos and Myanmar in 2022-2025 (MEA reply in Parliament, Feb 2026).
